\begin{abstract}
Repeated computational homogenization is formulated as a many-query
constitutive transfer problem for a fixed microstructure \(\G\) and varying
constituent vector \(\boldsymbol{\xi}\).  Its discrete
full-order response is the Schur map
\(\Cfom=D-B^TK^{-1}B\).  We compile the geometry-dependent affine blocks once
and enrich a Ritz space only with the six physical macroscopic-strain
responses at selected materials.  The resulting operator preserves the
variational hierarchy and interpolates the effective tensor and its affine
sensitivities at enrichment points.  A transverse--longitudinal factorization
of an isotropic Fourier reference compiles all six-output residual pairings
into \(BB\), \(BK\), and \(KK\) contractions.  Consequently, the matrix error
bound is evaluated online by reduced dense algebra, with no voxel-count
dependence at fixed rank.  For three-dimensional periodic short-fiber composites, a rank-168
operator reaches a maximum relative tensor error of
\(4.15\times10^{-5}\) over 128 independent materials.  On 16 high-precision
points, the direct bound has median effectivity 8.94; adding the exact
\(48\rightarrow168\) Schur drop to the bounded enriched tail gives zero
upper-bound violations and median effectivity 1.10.  The compiled operator then performs material
screening, sensitivity-guided conditional identification, and \(200\,000\)
uncertainty-propagation queries without further full-order solves.
\end{abstract}

\begin{highlights}
\item Homogenized stiffness is treated as a static constitutive transfer map.
\item Six-load tangential Ritz enrichment targets \(D-B^TK^{-1}B\), not all voxel fields.
\item A Ritz/Schur identity gives Loewner monotonicity and a residual error bound.
\item At fixed rank, two Fourier kernels remove voxel-count dependence online.
\item The reduced operator supports screening and rank-revealing conditional inversion.
\end{highlights}

\section{Introduction}

Short-fiber composites offer a large design space because constituent
properties, fiber content, orientation, aspect ratio, and spatial organization
can all be adjusted.  Their macroscopic behavior, however, cannot generally be
read from the constituent data alone.  It must be obtained from a periodic
microscopic boundary-value problem whose solution resolves the interaction
between material contrast and the heterogeneous geometry.  Computational
homogenization provides this connection and returns the effective stiffness
needed by structural analysis and material design.

The difficulty changes character when homogenization is embedded in a design
workflow.  A single accurate cell solve is no longer the final task.  Material
screening, inverse identification, optimization, and uncertainty propagation
may require thousands or hundreds of thousands of evaluations for the same
microstructure.  In three-dimensional elasticity, every candidate normally
requires six independent macroscopic load cases on a voxel grid with hundreds
of thousands or millions of unknowns.  Repeating that calculation while only
the constituent coefficients change makes the microscopic solver the dominant
computational bottleneck.

FFT homogenization reduces the cost of each periodic solve, while POD,
reduced-basis homogenization, and parametric model reduction compress families
of microscopic states
\cite{yvonnet2007r3m,fritzen2013hybrid,hernandez2014highperformance,
kunc2019finite,kochmann2019sparsefft,pham2024rbhm}.
Nevertheless, the engineering quantity ultimately queried is usually not the
entire fluctuation field.  It is the \(6\times6\) effective constitutive tensor,
and in identification problems also its derivatives with respect to the
material parameters.  A state-oriented approximation can therefore spend
offline information on field directions that have little influence on this
small output.  Moreover, a useful many-query model needs an error indicator
that can be evaluated without invoking the voxel fields or an FFT solve for
every candidate.

This paper asks whether the fixed microstructure can instead be compiled into
a reusable constitutive transfer operator.  The proposed answer starts from
the Schur-complement structure of linear computational homogenization.  The
geometry-dependent operators are assembled once; subsequent enrichment uses
only the six physical responses associated with the macroscopic strain space.
The reduced model is then constructed to preserve the Ritz energy hierarchy,
to interpolate constitutive sensitivities at selected materials, and to expose
a residual identity directly associated with the effective stiffness.

The central methodological step is an offline--online error estimator.  An
isotropic reference inverse separates into transverse and longitudinal Fourier
kernels, allowing all voxel-level residual contractions to be precomputed.
Online evaluation consequently involves only reduced dense matrices.  This
connects the physical six-load enrichment, the constitutive output, and the
error-control mechanism in one transfer formulation rather than treating them
as independent accelerations.

The specific methodological contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item a geometry-compiled, six-load Ritz transfer operator for the effective
Schur map, with constitutive and sensitivity interpolation at enrichment
points;
\item a matrix-valued residual compiler based on precomputed \(BB\), \(BK\),
and \(KK\) contractions and two isotropic Fourier kernels, making online error
control independent of the voxel count at fixed rank;
\item causal tensor- and sensitivity-oriented enrichment rules together with a
truth-free relative certificate stopping criterion; and
\item a reproducible assessment spanning high-precision energetic checks,
equal-budget controls, voxel and morphology variation, and three many-query
applications.
\end{enumerate}

Several properties used to analyze this construction are deliberately not
claimed as new.  Galerkin best approximation, compliant-output quadratic error,
coercivity-scaled residual bounds, and affine offline--online residual norms are
standard RB results
\cite{prudhomme2002reliable,quarteroni2011certified,boyaval2008homogenization}.
POD/RB acceleration of constitutive homogenization across linear and nonlinear
settings is likewise well established
\cite{yvonnet2007r3m,fritzen2013hybrid,hernandez2014highperformance,
kunc2019finite,michel2016variational,pham2024rbhm}.
Reduced Schur systems, Schur-complement perturbation bounds, and their
certification in static-condensation RB methods are also established
\cite{huynh2013scrbe,eftang2013port,smetana2015certification}.
Likewise, projection-based interpolation of parameter derivatives is established
in parametric model reduction \cite{baur2011interpolatory}, and variational
bounds for homogenized operators predate this work
\cite{kerfriden2014cre,vondrejc2015bounds}.  We state these classical results
only in their matrix-valued constitutive specialization and cite their proofs.
The novelty claim is therefore deliberately narrow.  To the authors'
knowledge, the closest antecedents do not compile all six-output residual
pairings of the homogenized constitutive map through the
transverse/longitudinal Fourier split into the dense \(BB\), \(BK\), and
\(KK\) arrays to obtain a voxel-free online matrix bound integrated with
physical tangential enrichment and constitutive sensitivity interpolation.

Table~\ref{tab:literature} positions this combination against the closest
antecedents.  POD, reduced bases, CRE certification, affine separation, and FFT
reduction are not claimed as isolated novelties.

\section{Periodic homogenization and constitutive transfer}
\label{sec:homogenization}

\subsection{Periodic cell problem and effective response}

Let \(Y\) be a periodic representative cell with local stiffness
\(\mathbb{C}(\boldsymbol{x};\boldsymbol{\xi},\G)\).  For a prescribed macroscopic strain
\(\overline{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}(g)\), represented by the Mandel vector
\(g\in\R^6\), the fluctuation \(u_g\) belongs to the periodic, zero-mean
compatible space and satisfies
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cell_problem}
\int_Y
\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(v):
\mathbb{C}(\boldsymbol{x};\boldsymbol{\xi},\G):
\left[\overline{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}(g)+\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(u_g)\right]
\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}=0
\qquad \forall v\in\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{per}},
\end{equation}
where
\[
\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(u)=\tfrac{1}{2}
\left(\nabla u+\nabla u^T\right),
\qquad
\langle u_g\rangle_Y=0.
\]
The effective constitutive tensor is defined by the volume-averaged stress,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:effective_response}
\Ceff(\boldsymbol{\xi};\G)g
=
\left\langle
\mathbb{C}(\boldsymbol{x};\boldsymbol{\xi},\G):
\left[\overline{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}(g)+\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(u_g)\right]
\right\rangle_Y .
\end{equation}
Equations~\eqref{eq:cell_problem}--\eqref{eq:effective_response} make the
separation central to the paper explicit: \(\G\)
fixes the phase and orientation fields, while \(\boldsymbol{\xi}\) changes the
constituent law over repeated queries.

\subsection{Discrete Schur-complement transfer representation}

Let \(u\in\R^n\) denote the compatible, zero-mean fluctuation field and
\(g\in\R^6\) a macroscopic Mandel strain vector.  After discretizing a linear
periodic cell problem, the microscopic energy is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:discrete_energy}
\mathcal{E}(u,g;\boldsymbol{\xi})
=
\frac{1}{2}u^TK(\boldsymbol{\xi})u
-u^TB(\boldsymbol{\xi})g
+\frac{1}{2}g^TD(\boldsymbol{\xi})g.
\end{equation}
Stationarity of Eq.~\eqref{eq:discrete_energy} with respect to the fluctuation,
applied to the six independent macroscopic strains, gives the full-order
response matrix \(X(\boldsymbol{\xi})\in\R^{n\times6}\):
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:fom_response}
K(\boldsymbol{\xi})X(\boldsymbol{\xi})=B(\boldsymbol{\xi}),
\end{equation}
Eliminating this response from Eq.~\eqref{eq:discrete_energy} then
produces the effective stiffness as the Schur complement
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:schur_transfer}
\boxed{
\Cfom(\boldsymbol{\xi})
=D(\boldsymbol{\xi})
-B(\boldsymbol{\xi})^T K(\boldsymbol{\xi})^{-1}B(\boldsymbol{\xi}).
}
\end{equation}
Hereafter, \(\Cfom\) denotes this converged discrete FFT response, whereas
\(\Ceff\) is reserved for the continuum quantity defined in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:effective_response}.  The ROM bound controls
\(\Cr-\Cfom\); voxel refinement is reported separately.
The formulation is first abstract: \(K(\boldsymbol{\xi})\) is symmetric
positive definite on the compatible fluctuation space and \(B\) contains the
six macroscopic right-hand sides.  Section~\ref{sec:protocol} defines the
short-fiber FFT realization of this structure.

\subsection{Material parameters and geometry-affine separation}

The physical material vector used throughout the study is
\[
\boldsymbol{\xi}
=
(E_m,\nu_m,E_L,E_T,G_{LT},\nu_{LT},\nu_{TT})\in\XiD.
\]
It contains the isotropic matrix Young modulus and Poisson ratio together with
five engineering constants for the transversely isotropic fiber.  We retain
this dimensional seven-parameter interface because it is the one used to
generate and validate all numerical evidence in the paper.  Linear elasticity
admits a reduction by the global elastic scale \(E_m\), as discussed in
Appendix~\ref{app:scale}, but that optional quotient is not used in the
reported campaigns.

For the isotropic-matrix/transversely-isotropic-fiber case, the local elastic
law is affine in seven coefficients
\[
\boldsymbol{\gamma}(\boldsymbol{\xi})
=
(\lambda_m,\mu_m,c_{TT},c_{TT}^{\times},c_{LT},c_{LL},g_{LT}),
\]
so that
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:affine_operators}
\begin{aligned}
K(\boldsymbol{\xi})
&=\sum_{q=1}^{Q}\gamma_q(\boldsymbol{\xi})K_q(\G),\qquad
B(\boldsymbol{\xi})
=\sum_{q=1}^{Q}\gamma_q(\boldsymbol{\xi})B_q(\G),\\
D(\boldsymbol{\xi})
&=\sum_{q=1}^{Q}\gamma_q(\boldsymbol{\xi})D_q(\G),
\qquad Q=7.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The microstructure \(\G\) contains phase, orientation, periodicity,
discretization, fiber volume fraction, aspect ratio, and topology.  The
operators \(K_q(\G)\), \(B_q(\G)\), and \(D_q(\G)\) are geometry-dependent and
are assembled once.  The online material dependence is carried only by
\(\gamma_q(\boldsymbol{\xi})\).  The separation in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:affine_operators} is the mechanism that turns
the Schur map in Eq.~\eqref{eq:schur_transfer} into an offline--online transfer
problem.

\section{Goal-oriented constitutive transfer reduction}
\label{sec:transfer}

The construction has one offline stage and one online stage.  Offline, the
continuous microstructure is rasterized, its affine geometry operators are
assembled, selected material points are solved under the six physical loads,
and the reduced and residual-contraction tensors are compiled.  Online, a new
physical material vector is converted into seven affine coefficients and only
small dense systems are evaluated:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:offline_online_flow}
\underbrace{
\G\longrightarrow\{K_q,B_q,D_q\}_{q=1}^{7}
\longrightarrow V_r
\longrightarrow\{BB,BK,KK\}
}_{\text{offline compilation}}
\qquad
\underbrace{
\boldsymbol{\xi}\longrightarrow\boldsymbol{\gamma}
\longrightarrow
\left(\Cr,\nabla_{\xi}\Cr,\eta_{\mathrm{cert}}\right)
}_{\text{online query}}.
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:offline_online_flow} also fixes the information boundary:
voxel fields are used during compilation, whereas a deployed query uses only
the saved reduced arrays.  Figure~\ref{fig:offline_online_architecture} makes
this separation concrete using the dimensions of the final operator: 28
six-load material responses produce a rank-168 space, while the online path
receives only a seven-parameter vector and the compiled reduced arrays.

\begin{figure*}
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.98\textwidth]{cmame_offline_online_architecture.pdf}
\caption{Data flow of the implemented constitutive-transfer method.  The upper
band contains the voxel-dependent offline operations and the actual dimensions
of the two-kernel contractions.  The lower band contains the online query,
which assembles and solves only a \(168\times168\) dense system and returns the
effective tensor, its material sensitivities, and the Schur bound.}
\label{fig:offline_online_architecture}
\end{figure*}

The reported basis is stopped at the scientific target \(10^{-4}\).  During
method validation this decision is checked on an untouched material set; once
the estimator is compiled, Eq.~\eqref{eq:relative_certificate_conversion}
provides the corresponding truth-free online stopping test.  Smaller errors
are retained for solver and identity diagnostics but do not motivate further
enrichment.

\subsection{Ritz projection and six-load tangential enrichment}

Let \(V_r\in\R^{n\times r}\) be a reduced Ritz basis built from selected
full-order response matrices.  Projection gives
\[
K_r(\boldsymbol{\xi})=V_r^TK(\boldsymbol{\xi})V_r,\qquad
B_r(\boldsymbol{\xi})=V_r^TB(\boldsymbol{\xi}),
\]
\[
X_r(\boldsymbol{\xi})
=V_rK_r(\boldsymbol{\xi})^{-1}B_r(\boldsymbol{\xi}),
\]
and substitution into the full Schur map~\eqref{eq:schur_transfer} gives the
reduced transfer output
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:reduced_transfer}
\boxed{
\Cr(\boldsymbol{\xi})
=D(\boldsymbol{\xi})
-B_r(\boldsymbol{\xi})^TK_r(\boldsymbol{\xi})^{-1}B_r(\boldsymbol{\xi}).
}
\end{equation}
Each enrichment material \(\boldsymbol{\xi}^{(j)}\) contributes the six
physical response columns from Eq.~\eqref{eq:fom_response},
\[
X(\boldsymbol{\xi}^{(j)})
=K(\boldsymbol{\xi}^{(j)})^{-1}B(\boldsymbol{\xi}^{(j)}),
\]
and the basis is updated as
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:tangential_enrichment}
V_{r+6}
=
\operatorname{orth}
\left[
V_r,\,
X(\boldsymbol{\xi}^{(j)})
\right].
\end{equation}
Thus, one material point corresponds transparently to six full-order load
solves and at most six new reduced directions.  We report the number of
material points, load solves, and reduced dimension separately in
Section~\ref{sec:results}.

The adaptive material-space loop is output-oriented:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:adaptive_selection}
\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\star}
=
\arg\max_{\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\Xi_{\mathrm{cand}}}
\eta_C(\boldsymbol{\xi}),
\end{equation}
where \(\eta_C\) is the tensor-oriented score assembled from the Schur
estimator derived in Section~\ref{sec:estimator}.
Only the maximizer \(\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\star}\) in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:adaptive_selection} is sent to the full-order FFT solver for the
next six-load tangential block through Eq.~\eqref{eq:tangential_enrichment}.

\section{Offline--online Schur error estimation}
\label{sec:estimator}

\subsection{Residual energy and two-kernel factorization}

Define the residual matrix
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:residual}
R(\boldsymbol{\xi})
=B(\boldsymbol{\xi})-K(\boldsymbol{\xi})X_r(\boldsymbol{\xi}).
\end{equation}
A field residual alone is not the target quantity of interest.  We instead
map Eq.~\eqref{eq:residual} back to the six-output constitutive space through
the positive-semidefinite matrix
\[
\mathcal{E}_M(\boldsymbol{\xi})
=R(\boldsymbol{\xi})^TM(\boldsymbol{\xi})^{-1}R(\boldsymbol{\xi}),
\]
where \(M\) is an isotropic Fourier reference operator.  For every nonzero
wave vector, its inverse separates exactly into transverse and longitudinal
traction kernels.  This Green-operator decomposition is classical
\cite{moulinec1998numerical,vondrejc2014fft}; the proposition below concerns
the subsequent pairwise compilation, not the decomposition itself:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:two_kernel_inverse}
M^{-1}=\mu_0^{-1}P_T+(\lambda_0+2\mu_0)^{-1}P_L.
\end{equation}
Pairwise Gram precomputation for affine residual norms is also standard in
offline--online RB methods
\cite{prudhomme2002reliable,quarteroni2011certified}.  The construction below
specializes that principle simultaneously to the six constitutive columns and
to the two physical Fourier kernels.
Using the decomposition~\eqref{eq:two_kernel_inverse}, and writing
\(X_r=V_rY_r\) and
\(R=\sum_p\gamma_p(B_p-K_pV_rY_r)\), we precompute, for
\(\alpha\in\{T,L\}\),
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:compiled_contractions}
BB^\alpha_{pq}=B_p^TP_\alpha B_q,\quad
BK^\alpha_{pq}=B_p^TP_\alpha K_qV_r,\quad
KK^\alpha_{pq}=V_r^TK_p^TP_\alpha K_qV_r.
\end{equation}
The online kernel matrix is then assembled as
\begin{align}
E_\alpha={}&\sum_{p,q}\gamma_p\gamma_q
\bigl(BB^\alpha_{pq}-BK^\alpha_{pq}Y_r
-Y_r^T(BK^\alpha_{qp})^T
+Y_r^TKK^\alpha_{pq}Y_r\bigr),
\label{eq:kernel_contraction}\\
R^TM^{-1}R={}&\mu_0^{-1}E_T
+(\lambda_0+2\mu_0)^{-1}E_L.
\label{eq:kernel_recombination}
\end{align}
\begin{proposition}[Exact six-output two-kernel compilation]
\label{prop:two_kernel_compilation}
Under the affine residual decomposition above and an isotropic reference
operator, Eqs.~\eqref{eq:compiled_contractions}--\eqref{eq:kernel_recombination}
recover \(R^TM^{-1}R\) exactly, up to floating-point arithmetic.  After the
reduced solve, their evaluation costs
\(O(Q^2r^2+Q^2r n_g)\) operations with \(n_g=6\), stores
\(O(Q^2r^2)\) numbers, and has no explicit voxel-count dependence for fixed
\(r\).
\end{proposition}

Including affine reduced assembly and a dense factorization, one transfer
query costs \(O(Qr^2+r^3+Q^2r^2)\) for fixed \(n_g=6\).  A hierarchical query
repeats this work for its two ranks; neither expression depends on
\(N_{\mathrm{vox}}\) once those ranks have been compiled.

Appendix~\ref{app:offlineonline} proves
Proposition~\ref{prop:two_kernel_compilation}.  The compiled contractions are
stored as \texttt{online\_estimator.npz} beside the reduced operators;
direct-Fourier and dense evaluations are compared automatically during
compilation.

\subsection{Ritz/Schur identity and Loewner hierarchy}

The following statement is the standard Galerkin energy identity for a
compliant output, written columnwise for the matrix-valued constitutive Schur
map \cite{prudhomme2002reliable,quarteroni2011certified}.  Its use in
homogenization and the associated energetic bounds are also classical
\cite{boyaval2008homogenization,kerfriden2014cre}; reduced Schur complements
and their error control also appear in static-condensation RB methods
\cite{huynh2013scrbe,eftang2013port,smetana2015certification}.  We record the specialization
because it exposes the six-output residual matrix used by the proposed
compiler; the identity itself is not a novelty claim.

For \(K(\boldsymbol{\xi})\) symmetric positive definite on the compatible
fluctuation space and \(X_r\) the Ritz projection of
\(X=K^{-1}B\) onto \(V_r\), the cited classical identity gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ritz_schur_identity}
\Delta C_r(\boldsymbol{\xi})
:=\Cr(\boldsymbol{\xi})-\Cfom(\boldsymbol{\xi})
=
(X-X_r)^TK(X-X_r)
=
R^TK^{-1}R
\succeq0.
\end{equation}
For nested spaces \(V_r\subset V_s\), the same classical variational
restriction gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:loewner_hierarchy}
\Cr(\boldsymbol{\xi})
\succeq
C_s^{\mathrm{eff}}(\boldsymbol{\xi})
\succeq
\Cfom(\boldsymbol{\xi}),
\end{equation}
a Loewner-monotone constitutive hierarchy.  This is stronger than scalar error
decay: the reduced stiffnesses approach the full-order stiffness from the
energy-upper side.

\subsection{Beta-scaled bound and online reference selection}

If the reference operator satisfies
\begin{equation}
K(\boldsymbol{\xi})\succeq
\beta(\boldsymbol{\xi})M(\boldsymbol{\xi}),\qquad
\beta(\boldsymbol{\xi})>0,
\end{equation}
then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:matrix_bound}
0\preceq
\Delta C_r(\boldsymbol{\xi})
\preceq
U_r(\boldsymbol{\xi})
:=
\beta(\boldsymbol{\xi})^{-1}
R(\boldsymbol{\xi})^TM(\boldsymbol{\xi})^{-1}R(\boldsymbol{\xi}),
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:frobenius_bound}
\|\Cr(\boldsymbol{\xi})-\Cfom(\boldsymbol{\xi})\|_F
\le
\eta_{\mathrm{cert}}(\boldsymbol{\xi})
:=
\|U_r(\boldsymbol{\xi})\|_F.
\end{equation}
The matrix inequality~\eqref{eq:matrix_bound} is the operator form of the
standard coercivity-scaled RB residual bound
\cite{prudhomme2002reliable,quarteroni2011certified};
Eq.~\eqref{eq:frobenius_bound} is its scalar reporting form.  The contribution
here is not the coercivity argument, but its simultaneous evaluation for all
six constitutive outputs by the compiled two-kernel contractions.
For validation only, its scalar effectivity index is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:effectivity}
\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{eff}}
=\frac{\eta_{\mathrm{cert}}}
{\|\Cr-\Cfom\|_F},
\end{equation}
which is at least one for a valid upper bound.  It measures sharpness but is
not used by the online stopping rule.

The direct bound can be sharpened without changing its reference operator.
For two nested Ritz spaces \(V_r\subset V_s\), define the exactly computable
reduced Schur drop \(D_{r,s}=C_r^{\mathrm{eff}}-C_s^{\mathrm{eff}}\).  If
\(U_s\) is any valid matrix upper bound for the enriched tail
\(C_s^{\mathrm{eff}}-\Cfom\), then the hierarchy in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:loewner_hierarchy} gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:hierarchical_bound}
0\preceq D_{r,s}
\preceq \Delta C_r
=D_{r,s}+\Delta C_s
\preceq D_{r,s}+U_s
=:U_{r\leftarrow s}^{\mathrm{hier}}.
\end{equation}
Hierarchical reduced estimators based on two nested approximations are
established in model reduction \cite{hain2019hierarchical}.  Here no saturation
assumption is needed because the remaining enriched error is not discarded: it
is bounded by the same coercivity-scaled two-kernel construction.  Evaluation
requires two small reduced solves and the compiled estimator at level \(s\),
but still contains no voxel field or online FFT.

For a stopping criterion that remains meaningful when the observed error is
very small, define the computable relative certificate width
\begin{equation}
\delta_r(\boldsymbol{\xi})
=\frac{\|U_r(\boldsymbol{\xi})\|_F}{\|\Cr(\boldsymbol{\xi})\|_F}.
\end{equation}
Here \(U_r\) may be the direct matrix bound in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:matrix_bound} or the hierarchical bound in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:hierarchical_bound}.
Because \(\|\Cfom\|_F\ge
(1-\delta_r)\|\Cr\|_F\), every \(\delta_r<1\) gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:relative_certificate_conversion}
\frac{\|\Cr-\Cfom\|_F}{\|\Cfom\|_F}
\le \frac{\delta_r}{1-\delta_r}.
\end{equation}
Thus the discrete-ROM target \(10^{-4}\) is guaranteed whenever
\(\delta_r\le10^{-4}/(1+10^{-4})\).  This rule uses no full-order validation
error and is therefore also the causal stopping rule for auxiliary enrichment.
Effectivity remains a sharpness diagnostic, but it is not used as a stopping
quantity when its true-error denominator lies far below the adopted accuracy
floor.

For each material, 129 reproducible isotropic reference shapes are tested and
the valid reference minimizing the Frobenius bound is selected.  The coercivity
factor is the minimum generalized eigenvalue over the matrix and fiber local
stiffnesses, with a conservative numerical margin; its derivation is given in
Appendix~\ref{app:beta}.  The high-precision study in
Section~\ref{sec:results_bound} compares the direct \(r=48\) bound with the
hierarchical \(48\rightarrow168\) bound.  SCM coercivity surrogates
\cite{huynh2007scm} and anisotropic reference operators are viable ways to
tighten the residual tail, but the ablation shows that they are unnecessary to
meet the target effectivity here.  The bound controls reduction error with
respect to the discrete FFT problem, not voxel discretization or the continuum
limit.

\section{Constitutive sensitivities and tangential Hermite interpolation}
\label{sec:sensitivities}

Differentiating the reduced Schur map~\eqref{eq:reduced_transfer} gives the
affine coefficient sensitivities
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:constitutive_sensitivity}
\boxed{
\frac{\partial \Cr}{\partial \gamma_q}
=
D_q
-B_q^TX_r
-X_r^TB_q
+X_r^TK_qX_r .
}
\end{equation}
Physical derivatives with respect to
\((E_m,\nu_m,E_L,E_T,G_{LT},\nu_{LT},\nu_{TT})\) follow by the chain rule
through \(\boldsymbol{\gamma}(\boldsymbol{\xi})\); the coefficient map is
listed explicitly in Appendix~\ref{app:affine}.  Sensitivities of homogenized
elastic properties and reduced constitutive surrogates with parameter
derivatives are established topics
\cite{kaminski2003sensitivity,guo2021constitutive,lukes2026sensitivity}; neither sensitivity
analysis nor its use in inverse problems is claimed here in isolation.

Parameter-response and parameter-derivative matching under projection-space
containment are established results in interpolatory parametric model
reduction \cite{baur2011interpolatory}.  Applied to the static symmetric map
\(D-B^TK^{-1}B\), the cited result states that if a material point
\(\boldsymbol{\xi}^{(j)}\) has been used for tangential enrichment and the six
full-order response columns \(X(\boldsymbol{\xi}^{(j)})\) are contained in
\(V_r\), then

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:hermite_tensor}
\Cr(\boldsymbol{\xi}^{(j)})
=\Cfom(\boldsymbol{\xi}^{(j)}).
\end{equation}
Moreover, for every affine coefficient \(\gamma_q\),
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:hermite_sensitivity}
\left.
\frac{\partial \Cr}{\partial\gamma_q}
\right|_{\boldsymbol{\xi}^{(j)}}
=
\left.
\frac{\partial \Cfom}{\partial\gamma_q}
\right|_{\boldsymbol{\xi}^{(j)}}.
\end{equation}
No derivative snapshots are required because the projection-space containment
condition is already satisfied by the six physical response columns.

Equations~\eqref{eq:hermite_tensor}--\eqref{eq:hermite_sensitivity} explain why
the six-load snapshots are more than training
vectors: they impose a local constitutive Hermite interpolation condition on
the Schur-complement transfer map.  Sensitivity-aware enrichment can therefore
be treated as a separate mode for inverse identification and optimization,
where the target quantity is \((\Cfom,\nabla_{\xi}\Cfom)\), not only \(\Cfom\).

For the causal comparison, let \([a_j,b_j]\) be the interval of parameter
\(\xi_j\) and let \(\eta_C=\|U_r\|_F/\|\Cr\|_F\) denote the relative tensor
score.  The sensitivity score and the mixed selection score are defined by
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sensitivity_selection_score}
\eta_{\nabla C}(\boldsymbol{\xi})
=\max_j (b_j-a_j)
\left\|\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi_j}
\left(\frac{R^TM^{-1}R}{\|\Cr\|_F}\right)\right\|_F,
\qquad
\eta_{\mathrm{mix}}
=\sqrt{\eta_C^2+\eta_{\nabla C}^2}.
\end{equation}
The optimized reference is used at each evaluation, and the derivative in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:sensitivity_selection_score} is approximated by centered
differences with relative step \(10^{-3}\), using only repeated dense
evaluations of the compiled estimator.  The factor \(b_j-a_j\) removes the
units of each physical coordinate.  Thus this rule targets rapid variation of
the residual-energy matrix without candidate FOM sensitivities, voxel fields,
or online FFTs.
In the comparison below, standard energy greedy maximizes
\(\|R^TM^{-1}R\|_F/\|\Cr\|_F\), QoI/Schur greedy maximizes \(\eta_C\), and the
sensitivity-aware rule maximizes \(\eta_{\mathrm{mix}}\).

\section{FFT implementation and numerical protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

The full-order baseline is an FFTHomPy/CuPy periodic Fourier--Galerkin solve
with six independent 3D macroscopic load cases.  Three principal,
noninterchangeable profiles are used in the paper: truth computations use
\texttt{float64} and relative tolerance \(10^{-10}\), the strict snapshot
audit uses \texttt{float64} and \(10^{-8}\), and the economical production
basis pass uses the timing profile with \texttt{float32} and \(10^{-5}\).  An
additional \texttt{rom\_floor} profile (\texttt{float32}, \(10^{-4}\)) is
reserved for rapid feasibility pilots and is not used for the final truth
comparison.  The economical profiles are used only to construct the offline
basis; their error is checked against independent truth solves and the strict
snapshot profile remains available through
\texttt{--basis-profile snapshot}.  Every solve must declare convergence and
records the relative residual of each load, iteration count, symmetry defect,
and minimum effective-tensor eigenvalue.  Failed, incomplete, non-SPD, or
nonconverged solves abort the campaign.

The implementation was checked on a homogeneous cell and an analytical 3D
two-phase laminate, including Mandel energy equivalence, compatibility,
projected equilibrium, periodicity, and Hill--Mandel consistency.  The
homogeneous-cell relative error is \(4.63\times10^{-16}\).  All 16 independent
truth solves used for the bound converged, with maximum relative residual
\(9.96\times10^{-11}\) and at most 198 CG iterations.  A separate refinement
uses the same continuous 96-fiber geometry at \(48^3,64^3,91^3,128^3\), and a
geometry campaign varies the microstructure while keeping the box and
\(91^3\) grid fixed.  Table~\ref{tab:geometry} summarizes the
baseline discretization, while Figure~\ref{fig:geometry} connects the continuous
periodic fibers to their voxelization and realized orientation statistics.

The validation metric is the relative Frobenius tensor error
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:relative_tensor_error}
e_{\mathrm{rel}}
=
\frac{\|\Cr(\boldsymbol{\xi})-\Cfom(\boldsymbol{\xi})\|_F}
{\|\Cfom(\boldsymbol{\xi})\|_F}.
\end{equation}
The present study uses \(10^{-4}\) in Eq.~\eqref{eq:relative_tensor_error} as
the scientific reporting floor.  Values near \(10^{-7}\) or \(10^{-8}\) are
retained as algebraic and solver diagnostics, but they do not trigger
additional enrichment once the scientific target has been reached.  Campaign
manifests and manuscript build commands are documented in
Appendix~\ref{app:repro}.

The equal-budget comparator is causal by construction.  A 4096-point Sobol
candidate pool, 256 untouched validation materials, ten random schedules, and
budgets of \(1,2,4,6,8,10,15,20\) materials are frozen before evaluation.
Sobol+POD, random, standard energy greedy, QoI/Schur greedy, and a
sensitivity-aware Schur rule each pay six full-order loads per selected
material.  Candidate full-order errors are never available to a selection
rule.  Mean, 95th-percentile, and maximum errors are evaluated on the same 256
truth tensors and supplemented by paired bootstrap intervals.  As a
nonintrusive control, an RBF predicts the 21 independent entries of the matrix
logarithm of \(\Cfom\) in normalized seven-parameter coordinates.
Gaussian, inverse-multiquadric, and inverse-quadratic kernels, five shape
scales, and four smoothing levels are selected by leave-one-out error using
only the available training tensors.  Exponentiation guarantees an SPD
prediction; this is the classical log-Euclidean construction
\cite{arsigny2007geometric}, not a claimed contribution.

\subsection{Offline--online bound and Loewner hierarchy}
\label{sec:results_bound}

The dense two-kernel estimator and the direct Fourier implementation agree in
\texttt{float64}.  Their maximum relative discrepancy is
\(9.12\times10^{-12}\) at \(r=48\); at \(r=168\) it is
\(2.17\times10^{-9}\) because the reference energy is much smaller, while the
absolute discrepancy remains \(9.39\times10^{-14}\).  Both satisfy the
\(10^{-8}\) algebraic diagnostic tolerance.
For 16 independent high-precision materials, all error matrices
\(C_{48}^{\mathrm{eff}}-\Cfom\) are positive definite; their minimum eigenvalue over
the campaign is \(3.17\times10^{-4}\), confirming the hierarchy in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:loewner_hierarchy}.  The optimized 129-reference bound has
zero strict Loewner violations and minimum eigenvalue
\(2.53\times10^{-3}\) for the direct gap
\(U_{48}-(C_{48}^{\mathrm{eff}}-\Cfom)\).  Its scalar effectivity from
Eq.~\eqref{eq:effectivity}
ranges from 2.86 to 25.51, with median 8.94; optimizing convex combinations of
the 129 valid references selects one active reference and does not improve that
median.

Equation~\eqref{eq:hierarchical_bound} changes the task from bounding the full
\(r=48\) error to bounding only the enriched \(r=168\) tail.  That tail is a
median 0.855\% of the \(r=48\) error norm.  Adding its valid two-kernel upper
bound to the exact
\(C_{48}^{\mathrm{eff}}-C_{168}^{\mathrm{eff}}\) drop gives zero strict violations, a
minimum Loewner gap \(3.45\times10^{-5}\), and effectivity between 1.012 and
1.411, with median 1.098 and 95th percentile 1.388.  Thus the median
below five is met without SCM or an anisotropic reference.  With one BLAS
thread, median tail-estimator time over the 16 strict rows is 1.90 ms.  A
separate 4096-material benchmark of the complete two-ROM bounded query takes
6.64 s in total, with median 1.53 ms and 95th percentile 1.95 ms.
This sharp bridge controls the \(r=48\) output while using \(r=168\) as an
auxiliary tail space.  A separate, predeclared test of the deployed \(r=168\)
operator added 20 materials selected only by the computable certificate width,
producing an \(r=288\) auxiliary space.  On 16 \texttt{float64},
\(\mathrm{rtol}=10^{-12}\) truths, its upper gap has zero strict violations.
Over 4096 candidates, 3988 implied relative bounds satisfy \(10^{-4}\), with
median, 95th-percentile, and maximum values
\(1.87\times10^{-5}\), \(7.36\times10^{-5}\), and
\(4.09\times10^{-4}\).  The auxiliary level does not sharpen the final-level
effectivity, whose median is 13.38, and tightening the truth solve does not
remove lower-hierarchy sign changes at the \(10^{-5}\) error scale.  We
therefore retain \(r=168\), already below the independent validation target,
as the deployed operator and do not use ``Certified'' in the title.
Figure~\ref{fig:bound_reliability} shows the direct ablation and hierarchical

\subsubsection{Inverse identification}

The ROM sensitivities are first used as a rank-revealing diagnostic, following
standard sensitivity-based practical-identifiability analysis
\cite{brun2001practical}, rather than assuming that all seven material constants
can be inferred from one homogenized tensor.  For validation material 94, the
normalized \(21\times7\) Jacobian is algebraically full rank but has condition number
\(4.58\times10^4\); its two weakest right-singular directions are dominated by
the fiber Poisson ratios.  Consequently, the seven-unknown problem is not used
as the application claim.  Its complete noise and regularization stress test
is retained in Appendix~\ref{app:inverse_identifiability}.

We instead pose a conditional identification problem in which matrix
properties, \(G_{LT}\), and the two fiber Poisson ratios are characterized
independently, while \(E_L\) and \(E_T\) are inferred from the effective
tensor.  In normalized coordinates
\(z_I=(z_{E_L},z_{E_T})\), the ROM-only problem is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:conditional_inverse}
\min_{z_I\in[0,1]^2}
\frac{\|\Cr(z_I;z_{\mathrm{known}})-C^{\mathrm{obs}}\|_F^2}
{\|C^{\mathrm{obs}}\|_F^2}.
\end{equation}
The corresponding Jacobian condition number is approximately 15, versus
\(4.58\times10^4\) for seven unknowns.  Across ten independent symmetric
perturbations at each noise level, the median normalized RMSE in
\((E_L,E_T)\) is 0.0065, 0.0237, and 0.0508 for 0.1\%, 0.5\%, and 1\% tensor
noise, respectively; the corresponding 95th percentiles are 0.0129, 0.0716,
and 0.119.  All 30 four-start optimizations converge and require no new
full-order solve.  Figure~\ref{fig:conditional_inverse} and
Table~\ref{tab:conditional_inverse} report the recovery and conditioning.
The result supports conditional identification of observable parameters, not
unrestricted recovery of seven individual constants.

between different microstructures; each geometry is compiled independently.
Likewise, the seven physical material inputs are retained although global
elastic-scale homogeneity admits the six-dimensional quotient described in
Appendix~\ref{app:scale}.

The causal comparator supports no universal selector ranking, while the tuned
black-box control isolates the value of the intrusive Schur construction in
this data regime.  A cross-code FEM comparison is outside the present scope;
solver credibility is instead established by the homogeneous, laminate,
Mandel, equilibrium, compatibility, Hill--Mandel, voxel-refinement, and
inclusion-refinement checks, including classical Voigt/Reuss and hydrostatic
Hashin--Shtrikman bounds.

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

We formulated a goal-oriented constitutive transfer framework for parametric
computational homogenization.  It combines six-load physical tangential
enrichment, constitutive Hermite interpolation, a Ritz/Schur error identity,
and a transverse/longitudinal two-kernel estimator whose online complexity is
independent of voxel count at fixed rank.  The FFTHomPy/CuPy study closes solver convergence,
basic energetic identities, dense/direct estimator agreement, and a
zero-violation discrete bound validation.  The screening, identification, and
\(200\,000\)-query UQ studies in Section~\ref{sec:applications} demonstrate the
intended many-query use.  Inversion is restricted by a rank-revealing
sensitivity analysis to the conditionally observable pair \((E_L,E_T)\); the
ill-conditioned seven-unknown case is retained as a negative control.  The
bound is reliable and sharp after hierarchical
tail correction; its guarantee remains relative to the discrete FFT truth
problem.  The causal campaign and the time-matched Sobol controls reject
universal material-selection dominance: fixed Sobol+POD can give lower raw
tensor error when it receives the same offline time.  Selection is therefore
an ablation, whereas the transferable contribution is the structure-aware
operator, its Schur diagnostics, and its goal-oriented outputs.  The black-box
control isolates the benefit of that intrusive construction.  Voxel error
remains separate from ROM error, and every new geometry requires its own
offline compilation.

\section{Affine constitutive coefficients}
\label{app:affine}

For the isotropic matrix,
\[
\mu_m=\frac{E_m}{2(1+\nu_m)},\qquad
\lambda_m=\frac{E_m\nu_m}{(1+\nu_m)(1-2\nu_m)}.
\]
The local transversely isotropic fiber stiffness is expressed in Mandel
coordinates using five independent coefficients
\((c_{TT},c_{TT}^{\times},c_{LT},c_{LL},g_{LT})\).  For a voxel whose local
fiber direction is \(a(x)\), the local tensor basis is rotated into the global
frame before being accumulated into \(K_q(\G)\), \(B_q(\G)\), and \(D_q(\G)\).
The implementation uses Mandel shear normalization consistently in the
full-order tensors, reduced tensors, residual indicators, and generalized
eigenvalue calculations for \(\beta\).

\section{Local coercivity factor and reference selection}
\label{app:beta}

Residual bounds obtained by comparing the energy operator with a coercive
reference are standard in certified RB methodology
\cite{prudhomme2002reliable,quarteroni2011certified}.  Here the argument is
written in Loewner form because the output is the full effective tensor.

If \(M\) is symmetric positive definite and
\[
K\succeq\beta M,\qquad \beta>0,
\]
then
\[
K^{-1}\preceq\beta^{-1}M^{-1}.
\]
Combining this with the classical identity~\eqref{eq:ritz_schur_identity} gives
\[
0\preceq
\Cr-\Cfom
\preceq
\beta^{-1}R^TM^{-1}R.
\]
For the voxelwise elastic operator, it is sufficient to impose the local
generalized eigenvalue condition
\[
C(x;\boldsymbol{\xi})\succeq
\beta(\boldsymbol{\xi})C_0(\boldsymbol{\xi})
\quad\text{for every voxel }x.
\]
With an isotropic reference \(C_0\),
\[
\beta(\boldsymbol{\xi})
=
\min\{\lambda_{\min}(C_m,C_0),\lambda_{\min}(C_f,C_0)\}.
\]
The implementation evaluates these generalized spectra for 129 reference
shapes, multiplies \(\beta\) by \(1-10^{-10}\), and adds a conservative
iterative-truth guard based on the recorded full-order relative residual.  This
guard accounts for numerical comparison uncertainty; it is not a certificate
of continuum discretization error.

\section{Two-kernel offline--online factorization}
\label{app:offlineonline}

Let \(\widehat{\tau}(k)\) be the Fourier transform of an affine residual-stress
atom and \(n=k/\|k\|\).  Its traction is
\(t=\widehat{\tau}n\), with
\(t_L=(t\cdot n)n\) and \(t_T=t-t_L\).  For an isotropic reference medium,
Parseval's identity yields
\[
\langle r,M^{-1}r\rangle
=\sum_{k\ne0}\left(
\frac{\|t_T(k)\|^2}{\mu_0}
+\frac{\|t_L(k)\|^2}{\lambda_0+2\mu_0}
\right).
\]
The isotropic Fourier Green operator and its transverse/longitudinal split are
classical ingredients of FFT homogenization
\cite{moulinec1998numerical,vondrejc2014fft}.

\paragraph{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:two_kernel_compilation}.}
For a fixed reduced coordinate matrix \(Y=Y_r(\boldsymbol{\xi})\), define the
six-column affine residual atom
\[
S_p(Y)=B_p-K_pV_rY.
\]
Then \(R=\sum_p\gamma_pS_p(Y)\), and bilinearity gives, for
\(\alpha\in\{T,L\}\),
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:appendix_pairwise_expansion}
R^TP_\alpha R
=\sum_{p,q}\gamma_p\gamma_q S_p(Y)^TP_\alpha S_q(Y).
\end{equation}
Because \(P_\alpha=P_\alpha^T\), expanding one summand yields
\begin{align}
S_p(Y)^TP_\alpha S_q(Y)
={}&B_p^TP_\alpha B_q
-B_p^TP_\alpha K_qV_rY \notag\\
&-Y^TV_r^TK_p^TP_\alpha B_q
+Y^TV_r^TK_p^TP_\alpha K_qV_rY \notag\\
={}&BB^\alpha_{pq}-BK^\alpha_{pq}Y
-Y^T(BK^\alpha_{qp})^T+Y^TKK^\alpha_{pq}Y.
\label{eq:appendix_atom_expansion}
\end{align}
Substitution of Eq.~\eqref{eq:appendix_atom_expansion} into
Eq.~\eqref{eq:appendix_pairwise_expansion} gives
Eq.~\eqref{eq:kernel_contraction}; substitution of the classical orthogonal
split~\eqref{eq:two_kernel_inverse} gives
Eq.~\eqref{eq:kernel_recombination}.  Thus the dense and direct-Fourier forms
are algebraically identical.

Each \(BB\), \(BK\), and \(KK\) block contains only \(n_g\times n_g\),
\(n_g\times r\), and \(r\times r\) arrays, respectively.  Once those blocks
have been compiled, the pairwise contractions require
\(O(Q^2r^2+Q^2rn_g)\) arithmetic for fixed \(n_g=6\) and
\(O(Q^2r^2)\) storage.  Every FFT and voxel sum occurs in the construction of
the blocks, so neither online quantity contains \(N_{\mathrm{vox}}\).
\hfill\(\square\)

\section{Seven-parameter identifiability stress test}
\label{app:inverse_identifiability}

For completeness, all seven normalized material coordinates were first treated
as unknowns in
\[
\min_{z\in[0,1]^7}
\frac{\|\Cr(z)-C^{\mathrm{obs}}\|_F^2}
{\|C^{\mathrm{obs}}\|_F^2}
+\lambda_{\mathrm{reg}}\|z-\tfrac12\boldsymbol{1}\|_2^2,
\qquad
\lambda_{\mathrm{reg}}\in\{0,10^{-6},10^{-4},10^{-2}\}.
\]
Ten independent symmetric perturbations were used at each of the 0.1\%, 0.5\%,
and 1\% tensor-noise levels.  All 120 optimizations converged, but the best
median normalized seven-parameter RMSE values were 0.183, 0.227, and 0.239,
respectively.  At 0.1\% noise, for example, the recovered-tensor error remained
only \(4.44\times10^{-4}\).  Small output residuals therefore do not imply
stable recovery along weak Jacobian directions.  This negative control
motivates the conditional problem
in Eq.~\eqref{eq:conditional_inverse}; it is not counted as a successful
seven-parameter identification result.  The complete 120-run table is retained
in the supplementary campaign archive.

\section{Elastic-scale homogeneity}
\label{app:scale}

If every constituent stiffness is multiplied by \(s>0\), then
\(K,B,D\), \(\Cfom\), and \(\Ceff\) scale by \(s\), while the fluctuation response
\(K^{-1}B\) is invariant.  Hence one physical modulus could be factored out
and the shape of the transfer map represented by six nondimensional ratios.
This study deliberately retains the seven physical inputs because its
sampling, validation, inverse problem, and user-facing material interface were
defined in those variables.  No dimensionality-reduction novelty is claimed.
